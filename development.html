FIELD DEVELOPMENT PLANNING

Turn the subsurface picture
into a staged plan.

Knowing more about the ground is one part of development. The next question is how to use that knowledge to plan locations, sequence investment and decide what needs to happen first.

WHAT WE MEAN

Where could it go?
What should happen first?

Field Development Planning brings the subsurface interpretation into the wider project plan. Dwell helps compare development options, identify the most useful next investigations and organize the decisions that lead from an early concept to a more defined programme.

For a geothermal project, this might mean comparing areas for further well investigation. For exploration, it might mean prioritizing target areas and follow-up phases. The right plan depends on the resource, the evidence and your development goals.

THE PLANNING QUESTIONS

Connect what is below
with what happens above.

Locations & options

Compare conceptual target areas and investigation locations against the geological interpretation and available surface constraints.

Sequence & dependencies

Identify which surveys, tests or specialist studies should happen before the next investment decision.

Uncertainty & alternatives

Show how different geological outcomes could change the plan, and define when it should be revisited.

YOUR PLANNING PACKAGE

A roadmap with
clear decision points.

Deliverables are tailored to your stage and agreed scope. The package can bring together:

A shared starting point

  • Subsurface interpretation and data gaps.
  • Development objectives and assumptions.
  • Known access and infrastructure considerations.

Options to compare

  • Conceptual target and investigation areas.
  • Alternative development sequences.
  • Specialist inputs and unresolved constraints.

A staged path forward

  • Recommended investigations and priorities.
  • Decision gates and evidence requirements.
  • Triggers for updating the model and plan.
Part of a coordinated team. Dwell’s subsurface-led planning supports the wider development process. Detailed well design, production forecasting, facilities engineering, economic evaluation and approvals require the appropriate specialists and separately agreed scopes.
A PLAN THAT LEARNS

New evidence should
improve the next decision.

As surveys, drilling or testing add information, we can revisit the interpretation, compare it with the original assumptions and update the development priorities.
